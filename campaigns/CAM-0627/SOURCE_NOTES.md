# Source notes

Kakushadze and Serur, SSRN 3247865, Section 6.4 specifies buying the cheaper ETF and shorting the richer ETF when executable bid/ask prices diverge beyond a threshold, then liquidating when the reverse executable inequality indicates convergence. It emphasizes marketable fill-or-kill orders and ephemeral opportunities.

Marshall, Nguyen, and Visaltanachoti, *ETF Arbitrage: Intraday Evidence*, Journal of Banking & Finance 37 (2013), 3486-3498, studies two liquid S&P 500 ETFs. The authors report rapid correction and economically significant profit net of spreads, while documenting wider spreads, one-sided order imbalance, and higher liquidity risk around the opportunities. Those findings make synchronized bid/ask replay mandatory.

The modern adaptation normalizes price ratios causally because current ETF share-price levels are not equal. This is labeled an adaptation, not an untouched replication.
